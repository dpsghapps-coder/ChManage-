# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two groups, roughly equal in importance on mobile:

- **Church staff and committee leads** — office admins and volunteer leaders who manage members, events, attendance, and committees, often while away from a desk (on-site during services, meetings, or visits).
- **Congregation members** — using the self-service portal to check their own record, programmes, committee involvement, attendance, and submit requests (change of details, transfer, discipleship, baptism, marriage, etc.). Many skew older or less comfortable with apps in general.

The job to be done is administrative and record-keeping: finding a person or record quickly, marking attendance, filling a form correctly, checking a status — not browsing or discovery.

## Product Purpose

ChManage+ is a church management system (members, families, committees, events, meetings, newcomers, communion, and a member self-service portal). It exists to replace paper registers and spreadsheets with a single, reliable system that non-technical church staff and members can use correctly without training. Success looks like: staff trust the data enough to stop keeping parallel paper records, and members can self-serve simple requests without calling the office.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, simple. It should feel like a well-run parish office, not a startup dashboard: unhurried, plain-spoken, dependable. Nothing flashy, nothing that calls attention to itself as "an app."

## Anti-references

- Not a flashy consumer SaaS look (no gradient hero sections, no marketing-site flourishes, no playful illustrations).
- Not dense/cramped like a developer tool (Linear, Notion-style density is wrong here — this audience needs breathing room, not information density).
- Avoid anything that reads as "techy" or intimidating to a first-time, non-technical, possibly older user.

## Design Principles

- Design for the least tech-confident user in the room, not the most.
- One clear primary action per screen; never make the user guess what to tap.
- Forms must be forgiving: large tap targets, clear labels, visible progress, easy correction.
- Legibility over density: prioritize readable type and spacing over fitting more on screen.
- Consistency between the staff-facing screens and the member portal, so patterns learned in one transfer to the other.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Design explicitly for older and less tech-savvy users: WCAG AA minimum, generous tap targets (44px+), high-contrast text, no reliance on hover-only affordances, simple linear flows over complex nested navigation, and forgiving error states with plain-language guidance.
